1 · Switching apps in one press

The launcher costs two presses: open the grid, then pick. What removes a press is not a different menu — it is not having a menu. A rail that is always on screen is one press to anywhere. A hamburger drawer is the same two presses as today, just on the other side; it is drawn here so the trade is visible rather than argued. The cost of a rail is 40px of width, permanently — on the brief that is width taken from the photo.

2 · RECEIVED and SHARE are not apps

They are two directions of one thing: what came in and what goes out. Both are about the same queue of photos, and neither is a place you go — they are things you do to the brief you are looking at. Three destinations collapse to one, INTEL, and the launcher loses two entries.

3 · Sharing the kneeboard

Reworked three times and simpler each time. One press sends the card to the flight. No recipient picker: choosing who gets it is a real want, but it is not what makes this useful on day one, and every way of expressing it costs a control. No hold gesture anywhere — there is none on OpenKneeboard, which is the same reason a route step already ticks on a plain click; a control that works only on the desktop does not exist for a pilot in VR.

The lead ticks AAR and it ticks here. Your own ticks come back when the brief ends.
Live sync is not a third mechanism — it is brief mode applied to a card. Same /rt socket, same presenter lock, same bar naming who holds your view. Outside a brief the card is yours and so are your ticks.
Ships as: one press. SEND puts the card on every kneeboard on the net and names the count so it is never silent. Receiving is not an event — the card simply becomes the card, with one line of provenance. Live sync is brief mode, reusing its socket and its lock rather than growing a parallel one.

Deferred, not dropped: choosing recipients. It needs an addressed message family and relay-side filtering — enforced at the relay, never in the client, or a card has already crossed the wire to someone who was not meant to have it. Worth building when a twelve-ship needs to brief one element without the other; not worth a control before then.

Still to decide: whether a received card is kept after the mission or discarded with the brief. Everything here works either way.
