CARD — editing the data on the sheet

MOCKUP FOR APPROVAL · NOT BUILT YET

Today a card's data only comes from a .card.json. This makes the sheet itself the editor: turn EDIT on, click a value, type. The template does not change — you are filling in the boxes it defines, never moving or adding them, which is the same split that lets a card cross the wire as a few KB of data.

Every value maps back to exactly one place in the data, and that is what makes this possible. A cell is rendered from a template string like {comms.primary[2].freq}; I measured the three templates we have — 57 of 61 cells are a single token like that. The other four are joins: {alt} / {speed} on every route leg, and {flight.from} → {flight.to}. So the unit of editing is the token, not the cell: 24000 / 350 is two editable values with fixed text between them. One rule, no special cases, and nothing is un-editable just because the template formats it.

Edits in place, on the sheet — not in a side panel, so you are looking at the card as it will read. No on-screen keyboard: you type on a real one.

Why a MODE and not always-on click-to-edit. This sheet rides on a pilot's knee for a whole flight, and the ticks are already a plain click by your choice. If every value were live, one stray tap during a merge silently rewrites a frequency and nothing says so. EDIT is off by default, turns off when you leave CARD, and while it is on the ticks stop taking clicks — one thing a click can mean at a time.

2 · The keyboard does the moving

Filling a card is dozens of short values in a row, and reaching for the mouse between each one is the whole cost of the job. Click once to get in, then never touch the mouse again.

Arrows move by GRID position, not reading order, which is why they are worth having as well as Tab: inside a table, ↓ from an altitude lands on the next leg's altitude, not on its note. Where a block has no such neighbour — the header band, a prose list — ↑↓ fall back to the same thing Tab does.

3 · Where the change goes

An edit changes the card this app is holding, immediately and permanently — it survives a restart, the way a chosen template and ticked steps already do. The file you imported is never written to. You loaded it; it is yours; the app does not reach back into your folder and rewrite it. To get an edited card out as a file, EXPORT writes a fresh .card.json wherever you point it.

An edit does not travel by itself. Ticks cross because a checklist is a shared, live thing; a half-typed frequency is not. You edit, then you CAST — and the whole card goes as it always did. That also means editing a card a squadmate sent you is safe: it becomes your copy, and they see nothing until you send it back.

The one thing that genuinely conflicts with an existing decision. Brief mode has no TEXT tool, and the reason is written into the protocol: typing has no place in VR, and a tool that works for desktop pilots but not VR ones splits the tool set. Editing a card is typing. I think the rule survives, because the two are different activities — you write a card at a desk before you fly, and you draw on a photo while you are flying. Editing is planning; ink is briefing. So there is no on-screen keyboard in this design: you type on a real one, and a pilot in a headset fills the card in beforehand or takes one a squadmate casts. If you want EDIT usable in the OpenKneeboard tab too, say so — it changes the design and is worth knowing now.
What I have deliberately left out of this pass, and want your call on:
Adding and removing rows. Everything above edits values in boxes that already exist. Adding a route leg, or a fourth tanker, means inserting into an array the template repeats over — a different and larger mechanism (where does the new row go, what are its empty values, how do ticks reindex). Real need, worth its own pass, and I would rather ship value-editing working than both half-done.
Before I build:
