A BOTTOM BAR FOR ACTIONS

LEFT = WHERE YOU ARE · BOTTOM = WHAT YOU CAN DO HERE · CLICK ANYTHING

The split is the idea: the rail answers which app, the bottom bar answers what this app can do. Nothing about a photo belongs in the same row as SETUP, and nothing about navigation belongs next to a pen.

It is also not a new surface. The tool strip already sits along the bottom of the stage while presenting — this generalises that instead of adding to it, which is why PRESENT and the pen live in the same bar as RECEIVED and SHARE rather than floating over the photo.

Icons only. Three view keys as words cost 37px of strip in Italian and forced the crumb out; as icons they cost 30px each and the bar has room for twice as many. The bar is chrome, so it hides with everything else and the captured kneeboard is still only the photo.

No action bar at all. SETUP already has a save bar, and stacking a second empty row under it would cost 36px to say nothing. The bar is present only where an app has verbs — its absence is information too.
What this buys, concretely. The strip stops being a junk drawer: it goes back to saying only where you are and whether you are online, and it stops overflowing — three word-keys cost 37px in Italian and forced the crumb out, three icon-keys cost 90px in a bar with room for ten. The tool strip stops floating over the photo. And every app gets somewhere obvious to put its verbs, which CARD needs immediately and the roadmap's MAP and PLAN will need next.

The cost: 36–44px of height, permanently, on a surface whose whole point is the photo. It is chrome so capture-clean still hides it, but on screen it is real. The rail already took 46px of width; this takes height as well, and the two together are about 12% of the window.

Settled: icons only, everywhere — including the cast key on CARD, which is the SAME glyph as casting a photo because it is the same verb. One glyph, one binding, and nothing new to learn.

And the bar only exists where an app has verbs. SETUP shows none. That keeps the cost honest: 36px on the two surfaces that use it, nothing on the ones that do not — and a bar that is sometimes absent teaches the pilot that its presence means something.
